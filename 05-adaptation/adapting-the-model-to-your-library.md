# Adapting the Model to Your Library

This toolkit is intended to be adapted. Libraries should revise the examples, policies, tools, and scenarios to fit their own context.

## Start with local questions

Ask:

- What AI questions are staff already hearing?
- Which questions are difficult to answer?
- Which user groups are asking about AI?
- Which services are most affected?
- Where do staff feel least prepared?

## Identify local risk categories

Consider:

- Privacy
- Copyright
- Licensing
- Sensitive or restricted data
- Internal or non-public information
- Academic integrity
- Research integrity
- Scholarly publishing
- Public communications
- Institutional policy
- Approved tools
- Referral pathways

## Replace scenarios

Use examples from your own setting, such as:

- Student assignments
- Faculty research
- Graduate writing
- Literature reviews
- Internal documents
- Public-facing content
- Data analysis
- Scholarly publishing
- Licensed resources

## Insert local guidance

Add references to:

- Approved tools
- Institutional AI policies
- Privacy or security guidance
- Copyright or licensing contacts
- Scholarly communication support
- Research integrity guidance
- Teaching and learning guidance
- Public communication or clearance processes

## Keep the process

Assess → Design → Train → Evaluate → Update
